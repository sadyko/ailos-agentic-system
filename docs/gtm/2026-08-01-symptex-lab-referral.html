Symptex — Lab-Referral Wedge

Launch plan · 1 August 2026 · segment: a person in Tashkent holding a printed slip listing 4–6 analyses

Scope. This tests the lab-referral segment only — not Symptex as a whole. The doctor-visit booking business is out of scope and nothing here is a verdict on it.

NO

The wedge as stated — decouple find → compare → book for a lab referral — fails on three grounds, any one sufficient:

  1. The money row has no foundation. The two verified prices are different products: Gemotest ΟАК without leukocyte formula (29,000) vs lab-net ΟАК 25 parameters (75,000).
  2. The wedge relocates work rather than removing it. Activities 6–8 do not disappear; Symptex performs them, manually, with no fee and no contribution margin anywhere in the model.
  3. Who pays contradicts what the wedge does. The clinic pays — and is asked to fund the loss of its own captive-lab revenue.

The incumbent

Who they actually areNot offline clinics. The existing directories — med24.uz and clinics.uz — which already own the listings and the traffic.
Structural protectionLab chains cannot credibly compare themselves against competitors. That protection is real.
Likely responseThe directories add real prices and booking on top of what they already have. Fast, and cheap for them.
Fastest failureSix weeks. The channel is EasyMed's printed slip — the clinic's printer, workflow and paid software. The first head doctor who notices referrals leaving for outside labs demands the QR is removed, and wins, because they are EasyMed's paying customer. Channel and revenue are the same relationship.

Separate proposal — not part of the verdict

Offered because the analysis surfaced it, not to rescue the idea. It has not been run through the gates and no verdict attaches to it.

The chain's own ranking puts «cannot read the reference ranges» → «doctor interprets them» above the link the deck targets. It is 5 EIPs — joint highest effort in the chain — weakly bonded, and sits immediately adjacent to the only two activities anyone pays for. Interpreting a result is unambiguously value-creating, the cell the book's data values around six times higher than value-eroding work. Symptex already owns the asset: 423 published analysis articles explaining what tests mean.

That is the difference between «we save you a trip round three price lists» and «we tell you what your result means». The chain as mapped describes an errand; the patient's job is finding out what is wrong with them.

It also inverts the channel conflict. Explaining a result drives the patient back to the referring doctor rather than away from the clinic's lab — so instead of asking a clinic to fund its own revenue loss, it sends them a return consultation. The payer's incentive and the patient's interest point the same way, which is the thing the current model cannot say.

Needs a medical-liability answer before anything else — explaining results is closer to advice than to comparison shopping.
