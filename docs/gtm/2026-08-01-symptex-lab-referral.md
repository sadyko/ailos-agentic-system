# Symptex — Lab-Referral Wedge · Launch Plan

> Generated by the `value-chain-launch` skill, 2026-08-01. Method: Thales Teixeira, *Unlocking the
> Customer Value Chain* (2019).
>
> **Scope:** this tests the **lab-referral segment only** — not Symptex as a whole. The doctor-visit
> booking business is out of scope and nothing here is a verdict on it.

## Verdict: **NO** — on the stated wedge, for this segment

The wedge as stated (decouple find → compare → book for a lab referral) fails on three grounds, any
one of which is sufficient:

1. **The delta triple's money row has no foundation.** The only two prices verified are not the same
   product — Gemotest's *ОАК without leukocyte formula* (29,000) and lab-net's *ОАК 25 parameters*
   (75,000) are different tests. Every saving figure was a subtraction inside an unverified range.
2. **The wedge relocates work rather than removing it.** Activities 6–8 do not disappear; Symptex
   performs them, manually at first, with no fee, no unit cost, and no contribution margin anywhere
   in the model.
3. **Who pays contradicts what the wedge does.** Gate 5 says the clinic pays. Weak link 2 says the
   point is breaking the clinic's captive-lab default. The plan asks the clinic to fund the
   destruction of one of its own highest-margin lines.

**This is not a verdict on Symptex.** It is a verdict on attacking find/compare/book *for lab
referrals*, and the analysis surfaced a materially better wedge in the same chain — see the separate
proposal at the end, which is offered as a proposal, not folded into this verdict.

## The segment — Gate 1: PASS

A person in Tashkent leaving a consultation holding a printed slip listing 4–6 analyses.

Locatable three ways: physically (anyone leaving a clinic), digitally (they search exact test names,
which Symptex's 423 analysis articles target), and structurally — **EasyMed prints the slip**. The
trigger moment happens inside software the company already controls.

## The 2×2 — Gate 3: PASS

| | **Firm charges here** | **Firm charges nothing** |
|---|---|---|
| **Creates value** | 21, **22** — the fortress: interpreting the result and knowing what to do | 3, 5, 9, 11, 19, 23 |
| **Erodes value** | 16, 17 — vulnerable but violent | 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 20, 24 |

The stated wedge spans two cells: activities 5 and 9 create value, activities 6, 7, 8 and 10 erode
it. **Mixed — not the "value-creating decoupler" Slide 8 claims.** The investor deck's category
claim is not supported by this chain.

## Weak links, ranked

1. **Activities 6–8** — 23 min, 14 EIPs, all eroding, all weakly bonded. The largest effort cluster
   in the chain and nobody charges for it. *This is the wedge the deck targets.*
2. **Activity 3 → 5** — where the clinic's captive-lab default breaks.
3. **Activity 20 → 21/22** — not understanding the result, which currently forces a paid return
   visit. **Highest-value link in the chain, and the one adjacent to the only activities anyone
   pays for.**

The plan targeted #1 and ignored #3. The cold critic's sharpest finding: *"they identify it, and
then build somewhere else."*

## The delta triple

Two current behaviours, and the split between them is unmeasured — **the single number every
estimate below depends on**:

- **(a) Default-takers** go to the clinic's own lab. ~4 min, ~5 EIPs.
- **(b) Searchers** compare manually. 40 min, 29 EIPs.

**Versus the searcher:**

| | Manual | Symptex | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money | uncompared price | cheapest visible basket | **`?` — no valid figure exists** |
| Time | 40 min | ~5 min | **−35 min** |
| Effort | 29 EIPs | ~5 EIPs | **−24 EIPs** |

**Versus the default-taker:** +3 min, +3 EIPs, money `?`. A trade-off that only works if the money
saving is real, large, and visible before the click — and there is currently no evidence it is any
of the three.

**Reading:** two currencies improve for one behaviour and worsen for the other, and the currency
that would decide between them is unknown. Per the method, that is not a win.

Two further problems with the denominator, both from the critic:

- 35 minutes is removed from a chain that also contains 25 min travel, 20 min queue, 1–3 days of
  waiting and a 40-minute return consult. That is roughly **20% of active time and almost none of
  the elapsed pain.**
- **Travel is never subtracted.** Activity 13 costs ~10,000 UZS. A saving captured at a
  non-nearest lab can be entirely consumed by the round trip.

## The four layers — Gate 4: PASS, but the pass is circular

| Layer | Description | Who performs which activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Traditional | ask the doctor, go to the clinic's lab | clinic recommends · lab prices · lab books · lab delivers |
| 2 · Digital version | clinic puts its price list and a form on its own site | same parties, web instead of phone |
| 3 · Incremental | lab chain adds cart, home collection, e-results — **LIVE TODAY** at gemotest.uz and lab-net.uz | lab does everything, online |
| 4 · Symptex | one place to compare and book across providers | **cross-provider comparison moves to Symptex; collection and analysis stay with the lab** |

Something does move, and the reason is sound: no single provider can credibly compare itself against
competitors — the same structural fact that let Edmunds and TrueCar take comparison away from Ford.

**But the pass is circular.** Layer 3 is already live and captures the entire user-visible benefit
for anyone who has already chosen a lab. Layer 4 only beats it if cross-lab comparison is worth more
than each lab's own convenience — which is precisely the money delta that has no valid figure.

**And the neutrality claim is contested.** Symptex takes SaaS and per-booking fees from clinics and
has a sister product sold to the same clinics. TrueCar is dealer-funded and has been fought over
exactly this question for a decade. Inspected closely, the analogy cuts both ways.

## Who pays — Gate 5: PASS, with a structural contradiction

Named and live: clinic SaaS subscriptions, a per-booking fee charged to the clinic, clinic API. The
patient is never charged.

**The payer is not the user, so the clinic's chain matters:**

notices empty slots or a competitor on Google *(trigger)* → hears a patient found them online
*(trigger)* → asks what Symptex is *(evaluate)* → compares against doing nothing or their own
website *(evaluate)* → **decides whether the per-booking fee is worth it** *(choose — the approval
activity, and the decision-maker is the owner or head doctor, not the registrator who uses it)* →
signs up and pays *(purchase)* → registrator works the queue *(consume)* → sees bookings arrive
*(consume)* → renews or churns *(re-need)*.

**The contradiction:** Gate 5 says the clinic pays. Weak link 2 says the wedge breaks the clinic's
captive-lab default. The model asks a clinic to fund the loss of its own lab revenue. The clinic's
in-house lab margin — and its share of clinic revenue — is nowhere in the analysis, so the size of
the counter-incentive being provoked has never been estimated.

## Research

**Verified by direct fetch:**

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| clinics.uz | 407 clinics listed for blood tests. **Every price reads "цена по звонку."** No booking, phone numbers only. Ratings and review counts present. |
| gemotest.uz | ОАК without leukocyte formula = **29,000 UZS** + 11,000 collection · 1-day turnaround · cart · home collection · fasting instructions |
| lab-net.uz | ОАК 25 parameters = **75,000 UZS** · cart · home call |

**Unverified — flagged, not assumed:**

- Blood-test price range quoted as 23,000–175,000 UZS (average 57,000–59,000). From search
  summaries only. **Not usable as a basis for any saving calculation.**
- **med24.uz** claims 1,000 doctors, 230 clinics, "all types of analyses at 200+ laboratories",
  prices, reviews and online booking. **HTTP 503 on two attempts.** If real, it occupies this exact
  wedge. Two failed fetches from outside the country is not evidence of absence — geo-blocking and
  bot rules produce that routinely. **This is a one-phone-call check that has not been done.**
- Named Tashkent lab networks: SWISS LAB, TTD, LABNET, HAEMALAB, MEDIK-AS, VITROS, Intermed, Genex,
  AKFA Medline, Gemotest.

**A reading of clinics.uz that was missed.** The first draft read "407 clinics, no prices" as an
empty niche. The more parsimonious reading is the opposite: **407 independent revealed preferences
against publishing prices.** Someone already built the listing; the supply side declined to fill it
in. If that is what happened, the product cannot exist as designed.

## The incumbent's counter-move

Cell attacked is mostly *erodes + no charge* — the method predicts "they copy you, fast but cheap."
But the copier is not who the deck assumes:

- **Lab chains structurally cannot** offer neutral cross-lab comparison. Genuine protection.
- **Clinics have no incentive** and, in this model, an active disincentive.
- **The real threat is the existing directories** — med24.uz and clinics.uz — adding real prices and
  booking on top of listings and traffic they already own.

## What the cold critic found

Dispatched with the finished plan and no other context, instructed to kill it. Findings that changed
the verdict:

| Assumption | Evidence? | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Labs will publish a price feed to the thing built to commoditize them | **None** — and clinics.uz is evidence against | Load-bearing. If false, there is no product |
| The clinic will pay to route its patient to a competitor's lab | None | A contradiction, not a risk |
| A real price spread exists on **identical** tests | None — the two verified prices are different products | Load-bearing |
| "Booking" is an event that exists at Tashkent labs | **Never examined** | If labs are walk-in, there is no slot to sell and no fee event |
| Enough patients are searchers rather than default-takers | None | Every number depends on it |
| med24.uz is not already there | Two 503s | One phone call away |

**Fastest failure — six weeks, not six months.** The distribution channel is EasyMed's printed slip:
the clinic's printer, the clinic's workflow, the clinic's paid software. The first head doctor who
notices referrals leaving for outside labs demands the QR is removed — and wins, because they are
EasyMed's paying customer. **Channel and revenue are the same relationship**, so a channel conflict
is simultaneously a revenue event in the working business. No mitigation exists in this plan.

**Second failure mode — price integrity at the counter.** If labs refuse feeds, prices are scraped or
hand-maintained and go stale. A patient arrives quoting a Symptex price; reception says no. In a
trust product one wrong price is unrecoverable, and the failure happens in front of the referring
clinic.

## Checklist — this is a **test list, not a spend list**

Do not spend acquisition money on this wedge. Every item below is cheap and falsifiable, and four of
them can kill the idea in a week.

### Kill-or-continue tests

- [ ] Open **med24.uz** from inside Uzbekistan, on a phone. Does it show real per-test prices across
      multiple labs, with booking? — owner: founder — done when: screenshots exist — **proves: whether
      the wedge is already occupied**
- [ ] Price **one identical test** — same name, same panel definition — at five labs on the same day.
      — owner: founder — done when: five figures in a table — **proves: whether a real spread exists,
      the fact everything rests on**
- [ ] Ask **one lab commercial director** whether they will publish a price feed to a comparison
      platform. — owner: founder — done when: one clear answer — **proves: assumption A1, the one that
      kills the product outright**
- [ ] Establish whether Tashkent labs take **appointments at all**, or are walk-in. — owner: founder
      — done when: three labs asked directly — **proves: whether a per-booking fee has an event to
      attach to**

### If all four pass

- [ ] Measure the **(a)/(b) split** — of 30 people leaving a clinic with a slip, how many compare
      before going? — done when: 30 asked — proves: the size of the addressable behaviour
- [ ] Get **clinic in-house lab margin** and its share of clinic revenue — done when: two clinic
      owners answer honestly — proves: the size of the counter-incentive
- [ ] Ask **five EasyMed clinic owners** directly: would you accept a QR on your referral slip that
      sends patients to another lab? — done when: five answers — proves: whether the distribution
      channel survives contact with the payer
- [ ] Produce **unit economics**: per-booking fee, cost to price one basket manually, contribution
      margin — done when: three numbers exist — proves: whether the relocated work can be funded
- [ ] Check **regulatory position** for handling medical orders, identity and results under Uzbek law
      — done when: a lawyer has answered — proves: whether this is legal as designed

### Not to be started until the above are green

- [ ] Anything involving spend, engineering, or the EasyMed slip

# Separate proposal — not part of the verdict above

Offered because the analysis surfaced it, not to rescue the idea. **It has not been run through the
gates and no verdict attaches to it.**

The chain's own ranking puts **weak link 3 — activity 20 → 21/22** above the one the deck targets:

- Activity 20 (reading results, not understanding them) is 10 minutes and **5 EIPs** — the joint
  highest effort in the chain.
- It is **weakly bonded**, so it is separable.
- It sits immediately adjacent to activities 21 and 22 — **the only activities in the entire chain
  that anyone pays for.**
- Interpreting a result is unambiguously **value-creating**, which is the cell the book's own data
  values ~6× higher than value-eroding work.
- Symptex already owns the asset: **423 published analysis articles** explaining what tests mean.

That is the difference between "we save you a trip round three price lists" and "we tell you what
your result means" — the second is the job the patient actually has. The chain as mapped describes
an errand; the patient's job is finding out what is wrong with them.

It also inverts the channel conflict. Explaining a result **drives the patient back to the referring
doctor** (activity 21) rather than away from the clinic's lab — so instead of asking a clinic to
fund its own revenue loss, it sends them a return consultation. The payer's incentive and the
patient's interest point the same way, which is the thing the current model cannot say.

Running this through the gates properly is a separate exercise, and it needs a medical-liability
answer before anything else — explaining results is closer to advice than to comparison shopping.
